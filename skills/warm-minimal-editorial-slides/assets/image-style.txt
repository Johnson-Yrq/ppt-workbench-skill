Warm minimal editorial photography for a professional presentation. Create a real, life-size photograph, never an illustration, miniature, model, diorama, product icon or synthetic 3D scene. The surrounding page palette is warm off-white #EFEEEA and deep brown #4A4235, with taupe and muted sage accents. Preserve the photograph's natural tonal range while using a restrained warm-neutral grade: cream, oat, weathered wood, warm stone, olive-gray, soft brown and quiet charcoal. Avoid an orange or sepia wash, icy blue lighting, harsh crushed blacks, blown highlights, strong saturation and glossy advertising effects.

Use believable available light, soft window light or calm late-afternoon daylight. Maintain realistic texture, fine detail, natural skin tones and subtle contact shadows. Choose a considered editorial camera position and natural lens perspective, with uncluttered foregrounds, purposeful negative space and one clear subject. Let real architecture, window frames, tables, shelves, textiles and materials supply the geometry. Do not add decorative gradients, glassmorphism, floating objects, artificial studio pedestals, bounding boxes, rounded frames, graphic overlays or fake paper borders. The final photograph has a clean rectangular edge; page layout, blank margins and typography are added separately.

Choose the subject from the page's meaning: an inhabited architectural space, a quiet workplace, people engaged in real work, hands and tools, material details, an outdoor setting or a relevant everyday object. People should have natural posture and believable interactions, with correct anatomy and complete hands when visible. Avoid generic posed handshakes, exaggerated smiles, futuristic control rooms and staged corporate stock-photo gestures. Architecture and interiors should feel plausible and human-scale. Images are conceptual visual context, never evidence of a named company's actual offices, employees, customers or results.

Compose to the requested crop. Wide photographs can support an introduction or a horizontal services image strip; tall photographs can support an about page; a cover may use a photograph occupying half the page beside an independent deep-brown text field. For a contents page, keep a large photograph visually quiet next to a narrow editable text column. Generate only the photograph, not the slide or the adjacent text field. When several images are requested, make distinct, related photographs with consistent color and lighting, without merging them into one collage. Preserve generous subject clearance so crops remain usable.

No visible text, letters, numerals, signage, book titles, legible screens, logos, brands, watermarks, signatures or pseudo-writing. Prefer genuinely unmarked objects or angles that exclude such surfaces; do not replace writing with gibberish. Never bake in presentation headings, captions, bullets, page numbers, tables, charts, icons, metrics, UI panels or factual claims. All explanatory and numerical content is added later as editable slide elements. No remote resources or font dependencies are required by this visual style.
