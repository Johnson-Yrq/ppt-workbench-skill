Natural-colour documentary business photography for a warm beige and black presentation.

Create a specific photograph that supports the actual page: a small business workspace, a thoughtful team meeting, a person at work, a city facade, a retail encounter, a showroom or a tactile architectural interior. Prefer quiet daylight, believable skin and material texture, soft neutral surroundings and grounded human activity. Use a restrained natural palette of cream, wood, stone, muted green, warm gray and occasional subdued blue. The photo should remain recognisably photographic, with clear focal structure and sufficient detail for a large presentation crop.

For portraits, photograph one distinct adult per image, with an individual face, expression, clothing and workplace setting. A seated or standing waist-up portrait works well. Keep the face and shoulders within the central 65 percent so the editable slide can crop the photograph into a circle. Leave sensible headroom and avoid hands crossing the crop boundary. Use documentary expressions and natural light rather than beauty retouching, corporate stock-photo theatrics or identical poses. Generated people are fictional illustrative subjects; their images do not authenticate real employees or clients.

For architecture and business scenes, choose a clear view with a real spatial background. Match the requested landscape, square or portrait crop, keeping essential subjects away from extreme edges. A tall facade may become a circular crop; a team or channel scene may become a rectangular crop. Vary camera distance and composition across related images instead of repeating the same room, face or laptop scene.

Preserve the environment and natural colour. Do not isolate subjects on a beige background, remove backgrounds, add global sepia or grayscale filters, make miniature models, plastic dioramas, glossy renders or floating interface mockups. Do not bake circles, borders, slide layouts, labels, text, names, logos, watermarks, charts or decorative rings into the image. The editable slide owns its warm paper, fine lines, circular masks and all factual wording.

Use the same restrained light and colour language across a deck without forcing identical colour or framing. Image counts, subject matter and aspect ratios follow the page brief. A text-led cover, timeline, table or chart does not require a decorative photograph.
