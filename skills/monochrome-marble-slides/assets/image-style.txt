Natural-colour business photography and an original grayscale marble texture for an editorial black-and-white presentation.

For photographs, create a believable moment related to the specific page: an individual working at a laptop, a company founder in a professional setting, a small group discussing a project, a thoughtful portrait or a calm workplace with natural light. Preserve real skin tones, clothing colours and spatial backgrounds. Prefer documentary compositions, restrained expressions, soft window light and convincing textures. Match the requested portrait or landscape crop; keep important faces and gestures inside its safe area. Vary people, camera distance and setting across the deck rather than reusing the same face or room.

The photographs remain in natural colour within the black-and-white slide system. Do not apply a global grayscale or sepia filter to people and scenes. Avoid glossy three-dimensional objects, miniature dioramas, exaggerated stock-photo gestures, cutout portraits, blurred or faded edges and decorative shapes baked into the photo. Generated people are fictional illustrative subjects and must not be represented as verified employees, clients or founders of a real organisation.

When the brief explicitly requests the marble texture, create one original seamless-looking close-up stone surface: flowing black and white mineral veins, organic diagonal movement, fine natural grain and variation between solid dark bands and milky white stone. Photograph it straight on with even diffuse light; no perspective, props, bevelled slab edges, gold veining, text or logos. Supply the clean full-contrast grayscale source. The slide theme controls whether it appears as a faint full-page background, a narrow edge, a high-contrast strip or an offset rectangular backing behind a photograph.

The slide owns its white and near-black fields, native serif typography, page numbers, short rules, borders, graphs and marble placement. Do not generate complete slide layouts, diagrams, chart data, readable interfaces, personal names, captions, logos or watermarks inside imagery. Do not force unrelated photographs onto white or black backgrounds. Choose imagery only where it supports the page's meaning; textual, financial and diagram pages can remain image-free.
